Online, 10:00 to 12:00 (JST).

**Programme**

1. Research report. Jun Ogawa (University of Tokyo), "Generative AI and the construction of historical person data from source descriptions"
2. Project meeting

**Report.** Ogawa first surveyed recent developments in prosopographical data models. The field has been moving from the Factoid model, which records information as stated in primary sources, to the STAR model, which attaches a source and an authority to each assertion and can thus preserve contradictions between sources and degrees of uncertainty. Efforts to make existing datasets interoperable, such as SNAP, are also active. He then introduced four recent studies that use generative AI to extract information about persons from historical texts. In the second half he presented his own current research, in which an LLM extracts personal attributes, kinship and social relations, careers, and acts of benefaction from 56,918 Latin inscriptions of Roman North Africa. Historians design the schema, evaluate the extracted data both quantitatively and qualitatively, and revise the schema and prompts in an iterative cycle. This approach reaches an accuracy of about 90 per cent and is usable for identifying patterns.

**Project meeting.** As of 26 September there are 2,695 reviewed and 1,254 not yet reviewed XML files, 3,949 in all. When the entries of about 30 tokens are finished, work moves to entries of about 70 tokens and then about 130 tokens; to test whether the AI handles longer entries correctly, each member will prepare one reference encoding for each length. For the sake of efficiency and accuracy, the meeting suggested that the AI should no longer be asked to look up external reference identifiers. A research visit to Hamburg is planned for 21 and 22 November. One in-person meeting will be held between January and March, and next year the team plans about three meetings together with invited speakers.
