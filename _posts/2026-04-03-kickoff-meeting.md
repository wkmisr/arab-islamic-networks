The first meeting of the project set out the goals and the four-year plan, and fixed what the first year should achieve:

1. Establish a working method on a sample of 3,000 entries from *al-Ḍawʾ al-Lāmiʿ*.
2. Follow current developments in digital humanities at DH2026.
3. Set up the framework for international collaboration.
4. Recruit research assistants for the encoding of about 10,000 entries in the second year.

A working plan for the database was circulated. Every person receives an internal identifier of the form `AIND-Dxxxxx`, which serves as the hub linking to external resources such as Wikidata. People, institutions and places that are mentioned in the text but have no entry of their own are kept in a separate provisional list. Encoding proceeds in three phases: a first 500 entries to test the tagging rules, then up to 3,000 entries while the AI-assisted tagging is tuned, and finally the remaining entries with a model trained on the earlier work.
